# Kestrel: a design brief in the manner of Paul Rand

*https://kestrel.delivery · 2026-07-08 · produced by rand-brief*

## The name

A kestrel is the falcon that hovers. It fixes a point in the wind, holds still, then commits. Two hard syllables. A rare, angular K. The name hands us a bird, a fixed point, and the letter to draw them with. Few names give this much.

## The identity today

Today Kestrel wears a teal gradient swoosh. So has every courier since 1998.

One of seven met, two partial. The name is doing no work.

## The idea

The mark is the hover: a kestrel reduced to four strokes that read as a K. A courier sells one thing — a fixed point in moving air. The parcel arrives at the window you were given. The hovering bird is that promise, drawn. The K signs it.

## The mark

Draw it on a twelve-unit grid. The stem is the body. Two units wide, full height. The diagonals are wings, raised mid-hover, meeting the stem at 45°. Upper wing five units, lower three. That lopsidedness is the mnemonic. A child could redraw it, wings and all. In solid black, nothing is lost. At sixteen pixels, three strokes stay three strokes. On a truck, the bird lands before the letter does. Between wing and stem, the white space forms a wedge. It points where the parcel goes.

## Typography

Univers, bold, for the wordmark. Rand judged bold Univers far superior to Helvetica. The counters stay open. Body text is Caslon, a face he called readable and beautiful. Licensed: Univers Next and Adobe Caslon Pro. Free: Archivo and Libre Caslon Text. Rigor on the box. Warmth in the letter that rides with it.

## Color

Rust, `#B8441F`. The color of a kestrel's back. Black and white do the daily work. Rust does the finding. In a teal category, the one rust van is legible a block away.

## In use

Favicon: the K-bird alone, black on white. Lockup: mark, one stem-width of air, then the wordmark. Below 24 pixels the bird flies alone. On the van, the wedge points the direction of travel. Let the drivers notice it themselves.
